M24

Solving the Puzzle

The fundamental fact about M24

Any five of the 24 numbers can appear on any five balls.  Having pinned five numbers, the remaining 19 numbers can be arranged in 48 different ways.  If you get a total of seven numbers on the correct balls, the remaining 17 automatically will be where they belong, too.

The basic strategy

Find several combinations of moves that leave some numbers fixed while mixing up the rest.   You may find it useful to define those combinations as combo button definitions and study exactly what they do.   The explanation of combo buttons describes doing this with the Right+Swap combination.

Use combo moves, spins and swaps to move seven numbers, usually one at a time, to their correct balls.

Once you have five numbers in position, you may find that you must come up with some sequence that not only preserves the positions of those five numbers, but also moves both the last two numbers into position at once.  The trick is to find combo moves that work together well, preserving mostly the same positions.

It is also probably easiest if the seven balls you are targeting are next to one another.  For instance, if you are trying to get 1 through 7 in place, you need some moves that leave 2 through 7 undisturbed.  The trick is to find useful combo moves like that!  Some moves that only protect 2 through 6 or 3 through 7 might be helpful, too.

To get started you can easily to move 7 to the 1-ball;  if it is on the 0-ball, use Swap, and otherwise just spin it around the ring from wherever it is. Then use Right to move 7 into 2-ball in the "protected" group.  Next work on moving 6 to the 1-ball using only your special moves, and tick it in next to the 7, and so forth.

Acclaim for Solving Quickly

When you reach the Home position from a random puzzle, M24 will announce this with a sound (if you have sound effects enabled).  For a long solution, you will hear the "tada" sound, but if your solution is short enough, the crowd will cheer!

Notes for Math Geeks

M24 is an implementation of the Mathieu simple sporadic group M24 which is a 5-transitive group.  This is the property mentioned above that there is some puzzle position that places an arbitrary set of five of the 24 numbers on any given five balls.

There are 24 × 23 × 22 × 21 × 20 = 5,100,480 different ways to place five numbers on five balls.  The structure of M24 then constrains the remaining 17 numbers to one of 48 different arrangements, giving a total of 244,823,040 different puzzle positions.
