M12

Solving the Puzzle

The fundamental fact about M12

M12 is based on a 5-transitive group.  This means that any five numbers can appear on any five balls.  Having pinned five numbers, the remaining 19 numbers can be arranged in 48 different ways, so if you get a total of seven numbers on the correct balls, the remaining 17 will be where they belong, too.

The basic strategy

Find several combinations of moves that leave some numbers fixed while mixing up the rest.   It is very useful to define those combinations as combo button definitions and study exactly what they do.   The explanation of combo buttons describes doing this with the Right+Swap combination.

Use combo moves, spins and swaps to move seven numbers, usually one at a time, to their correct balls.   The trick is to find combo moves that work together well, preserving mostly the same positions.

You may find that once you have five numbers in position, you must come up with some sequence that not only preserves their positions, but also moves both the last two numbers into position at once.

It is also probably easiest if the seven balls you are targeting are next to one another.  For instance, if you are trying to get 1 through 7 in place, you can arrange to move 7 to the 1-ball, and then use Right to move it into the "protected" group.  Moving 7 to the 1-ball should be very easy; if it is on the 0-ball, use Swap, and otherwise just spin it around the ring from wherever it is.   Next work on moving 6 to the 1-ball using only moves that leave 2 through 7 undisturbed, and tick it in next to the 7, and so forth.

The trick is to find useful moves like that!  Some moves that only protect 2 through 6 or 3 through 7 might be helpful, too.

Acclaim for Solving Quickly

When you reach the Home position from a random puzzle, M12 will announce this with a sound (if you have sound effects enabled).  For a long solution, you will hear the "tada" sound, but if your solution is short enough, the crowd will cheer!
